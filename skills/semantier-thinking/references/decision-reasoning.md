# Decision — choose responsibly under constraints

**Lead task:** determine an action among options, including whether to defer. The decision belongs to the user or authorized owner, not the assistant.

## L1 — focused decision
Identify objective, nonnegotiable constraints and one key tradeoff. Choose among actually feasible options, or explain which preference would alter the ranking. Do not invent a utility weighting.

## L2 — structured decision
1. Frame the decision, owner, deadline, affected parties, and baseline (including no action/deferral when feasible).
2. Enumerate feasible options; reject options violating hard constraints *before* scoring preferences.
3. Separate evidence-based outcomes from value tradeoffs; compare cost, opportunity cost, benefits, exposure, uncertainty and important non-financial effects.
4. Classify reversibility in concrete undo time/cost. Reversible experiments merit lighter planning; irreversible commitments warrant stronger evidence and staged options.
5. Perform a sensitivity check on the assumption most likely to change the choice. Identify information worth obtaining before committing.
6. Make a justified conditional or final recommendation, limits, owner action and review condition. A recommendation does not authorize an actual transaction.

## L3 — consequential decision audit
- Show option-by-constraint pass/fail, provenance for each consequential estimate, base rates when known, time horizon, decision authority and stakeholder impacts.
- Test dominant/robust options, adverse scenarios, pre-mortem failure paths and explicit verification or rollback gates. Avoid a generic list of risks.
- Use probabilities/expected values **only with defensible inputs**; otherwise show ranges, break-even conditions or scenarios without fictitious precision.
- For normative conflict call [ethical reasoning](ethical-reasoning.md); for recurring feedback/incentive structure call [organizational reasoning](organizational-reasoning.md). Both are conditional.
- Stop when a decision is ready or when the genuine blocker is user values, missing evidence or legal/domain review.

**Research source:** Richard Paul and Linda Elder, *Critical Thinking: Tools for Taking Charge of Your Professional and Personal Life* (Chinese edition chapter 9). The four-part decision pattern covers recognition, options, evaluation and acting. It is not a mandatory mechanical checklist; appropriate alternatives and evidence depend on context.
