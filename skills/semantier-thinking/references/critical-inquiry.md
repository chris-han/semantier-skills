# Inquiry — understand and verify what may be true

**Lead task:** interpret a statement, investigate a claim, assess source quality or infer what follows from supplied evidence. Do not load this for writing a complete letter unless a material factual gap in the letter needs assessment.

## L1 — focused verification
1. Restate the *specific proposition*, its time/population/scope and what's already established.
2. Find the single most decision-changing source, assumption or inference; check it where evidence is accessible. Make a scoped conclusion with uncertainty if source access fails.

## L2 — structured inquiry
1. Separate observations, quoted source claims, hypotheses, premises, definitions, causal claims and judgments.
2. Seek direct/primary material and its dates, methodology, population and provenance; cross-check independently where needed. SIFT/lateral reading is useful for uncertain online provenance.
3. Test whether evidence actually supports the proposition; consider the strongest *plausible* competing explanation, effect of missing data and source incentives.
4. Distinguish descriptive (“what happened?”), predictive (“what may happen?”) and causal (“did X produce Y?”). Do not infer causation from correlation or before/after comparisons without relevant assumptions and design.
5. Deliver the strongest currently defensible conclusion and what could falsify or qualify it.

## L3 — auditable evidence analysis
- Mark the exact source region for each load-bearing claim (title, URL/file, section/page/line, date). Separate direct quotation from paraphrase.
- Build a minimal traceable claim map: stated proposition, supporting/contradicting/qualifying evidence, material unknown and any reconstructed **unstated** warrants. Evidence graph integrity is *not* factual truth.
- Examine sampling, experiment assignment, controls, base rate, statistical effect and uncertainty where relevant. Check whether positive, negative and boundary cases are in evidence.
- Test independent counterevidence and qualified alternatives; preserve genuine conflict rather than averaging incompatible results. No fake confidence percentages.
- If data sources are unavailable, label claims `UNVERIFIED`/`CANNOT_ASSESS`, not `FALSE`.

## Coach branch
Ask for one of: what was actually observed? what source could distinguish explanations? what assumption makes your conclusion follow? what would change your mind? Advance only after response; if the user wants direct answer, exit Coach.

**Common failures:** inference from one anecdote; news paraphrase treated as primary; many citations used as independent corroboration when they share one origin; common jargon masking disagreement over definition; presuming a claim is false merely because the argument offered is insufficient.
